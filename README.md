# Worldview Finder

Version 1.0.1 · [Worldview Finder on PaulLinehan.co](https://paullinehan.co/resources/worldview-finder/)

An interview you can run with an AI assistant. Start with a memory from your life. Over time, you can add the chapters of your story and put what you believe into your own words. You can start anywhere, skip anything, and stop whenever you want.

## Start here (about 15 minutes)

1. Open `interview-prompt.txt`, copy all of it, and paste it into a new chat in ChatGPT or Claude. That one file has the instructions and all 90 questions.
2. Send it. The assistant asks if you want it gentle or direct, then asks about one memory from your life. Do one scene, or up to three if you're on a roll. You can skip anything, ask to see all the questions, or stop whenever you want.
3. When you're done, say “Wrap up and give me my index, the archive entries from today, and a place to pick up next time.”
4. Save what it gives you. The **index** is a short note of where you left off. The **archive** is your answers word for word, with dates. The AI writes both. You just keep them somewhere you control.

You'll also get a short reflection, marked as the AI's guess. If your answers point to a story worth telling, it'll say so.

Prefer separate files? [PROMPT.md](PROMPT.md) has the instructions. The two question lists are [Life Story](LIFE-STORY-QUESTIONS.md) and [Worldview](WORLDVIEW-QUESTIONS.md). Paste or attach all three.

**Using Codex or Claude Code?** Unzip this folder and open it there. Say “Start my Worldview Finder interview.” The `AGENTS.md` file (and `CLAUDE.md`, which points to it) tells the assistant what to do. It asks before it saves anything, and it saves your index and archive to a folder you pick.

## Pick up where you left off

In a new chat, give the assistant the instructions and both banks, paste your latest index, and add any archive answers it needs. Say “Resume from my index.” The index points back to your full answers. Keep the full archive even when you paste only a few entries.

A Project keeps the instructions and your record in one place, so you don't paste them every time. It's optional. Upload only the record or excerpts you're comfortable storing with that provider. Check your account's sharing and data settings before you add anything sensitive. Keep your own copy.

### ChatGPT Projects

1. Choose **New project** in the sidebar. Open the project's **Project settings** from its more options menu and paste `PROMPT.md` into the project instructions.
2. Add both banks to the project sources. If you're resuming, add your latest index and the archive or the parts you want the assistant to use. Start a chat inside the project and ask it to start or resume the interview. [OpenAI's Projects guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10169521-projects-in-chatgpt)

### Claude Projects

1. Open **Projects** and choose **New Project**. Use **Set project instructions** to paste `PROMPT.md`, then save.
2. In the project's knowledge area, use **+** to add both banks and, when resuming, your latest index and relevant archive. If a Markdown file won't upload, save a plain-text `.txt` copy and upload that. Start or resume inside the project. [Claude's Projects guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9519177-how-can-i-create-and-manage-projects), [Claude's file guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8241126-upload-files-to-claude)

### Replace the record after a session

An attached project file doesn't update itself.

1. Save the updated index and append the new dated entries to your full archive. Check that the words and dates match what you want to keep.
2. Remove the old index and archive attachments from the project's sources or knowledge area and add the new files. If you upload just part of the archive, name the new file so you can tell which sessions it covers.
3. In the next chat, ask the assistant to read back the latest session date and where you left off. If it can't see the right version, paste the index and the needed answers directly.

Menu names and file support can vary by account and device. If Projects or uploads aren't available, use the paste steps above.

## Keep what you want

The [index template](INDEX-TEMPLATE.md) tracks where you are. The [archive template](ARCHIVE-TEMPLATE.md) keeps your dated answers in full. You can ask for a private one-page snapshot of beliefs you've stated or confirmed, a list of possible stories, a letter based on one story, or a separate context draft to review before you give it to another assistant.

Use nicknames or leave details out. If you speak your answers, check the transcript before treating it as your words. An interview can cover hard memories, and you can pass on any of them. It's for telling your story and working out what you believe. It's no substitute for therapy or a diagnosis.

The Life Story structure draws on Dan P. McAdams's *The Life Story Interview II* from the Foley Center for the Study of Lives at Northwestern University. The questions here use original wording. [Life Story Interview II](https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/4/3901/files/2020/11/The-Life-Story-Interview-II-2007.pdf)
